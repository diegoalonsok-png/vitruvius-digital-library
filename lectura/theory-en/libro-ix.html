Latín

Nobilibus athletis, qui Olympia, Pythia Isthmia, Nemea vicissent, Graecorum maiores ita magnos honores constituerunt, uti non modo in conventu stantes cum palma et corona ferant laudes, sed etiam, cum revertantur in suas civitates cum victoria, triumphantes quadrigis in moenia et in patrias invehantur e reque publica perpetua vita constitutis vectigalibus fruantur. Cum ergo id animadvertam, admiror, quid ita non scriptoribus eidem honores etiamque maiores sint tributi, qui infinitas utilitates aevo perpetuo omnibus gentibus praestant. Id enim magis erat institui dignum, quod athletae sua corpora exercitationibus efficiunt fortiora, scriptores non solum suos sensus, sed etiam omnium, <cum> libris ad discendum et animos exacuendos praeparant praecepta.

Traducción

The Greeks established such great honors for the noble athletes who had won Olympia, Isthmia, and Nemea, that they should not only stand in the assembly and bear praise with a palm and a crown, but also, when they returned to their cities with victory, be carried triumphantly in chariots into the walls and into their homelands, and enjoy the fruits established by the state for life. Therefore, when I observe this, I wonder why even greater honors should not be paid to writers, who have provided infinite benefits to all nations throughout all ages. For it was more worthy of being established that athletes make their bodies stronger by exercise, writers prepare not only their own senses, but also the precepts of all, <with> books for learning and sharpening the minds.

From which those who are satisfied from an early age with the abundance of learning, have the best of wise sense, and establish in their cities the morals of humanity, just rights, and laws, without which no city can be safe.

Latín

Et primum Platonis e multis ratiocinationibus utilissimis unam, quemadmodum ab eo explicata sit, ponam. Locus aut ager paribus lateribus si erit quadratus eumque oportuerit duplicare, quod opus fuerit genere numeri, quod multiplicationibus non invenitur, eo descriptionibus linearum emendatius reperitur. Est autem eius rei haec demonstratio. Quadratus locus, qui erit longus et latus pedes denos, efficit areae pedes C. Si ergo opus fuerit eum duplicare, id est pedum CC, item e paribus lateribus facere, quaerendum erit, quam magnum latus eius quadrati fiat, ut ex eo CC pedes duplicationibus areae respondeant. Id autem numero nemo potest invenire. Namque si XIIII constituentur, erunt multiplicati pedes CXCVI, si XV, pedes CCXXV.

Traducción

First I shall set out one of Plato’s many valuable demonstrations, as he himself expounded it. If a place or field is square, with equal sides, and it must be doubled, this cannot be achieved by any number through multiplication, but is obtained instead by the correct drawing of lines. And this is a demonstration of this. A square place, which is ten feet long and ten feet wide, makes an area of 100 feet. Therefore, if it is necessary to double it, to make it 200 feet, and also with equal sides, it will be necessary to find how large the side of its square is made, so that from it 200 feet correspond to the doublings of the area. But no one can find this numerically. For if 14 are formed, the multiplied feet will be 196, if 15, 225 feet.

Traducción

Since the result cannot be expressed as a whole-number measure, draw a diagonal from corner to corner of the square ten feet long and ten feet wide. This divides it into two equal triangles, each of fifty square feet. Construct a square with equal sides on the length of that diagonal. The smaller square contains two triangles of fifty square feet; the larger contains four of the same size and area. Plato thus explained the doubling by geometrical construction, as shown in the diagram drawn at the foot of the page.

Traducción

Later it was reported that some gold had been removed and an equal weight of silver mixed into the crown. Indignant at the deception, but unable to find a way of exposing the theft, Hiero asked Archimedes to investigate. While considering the problem, Archimedes happened to enter a bath. As he lowered himself into the tub, he noticed that the water flowing over its edge corresponded to the volume of his body submerged in it. Realizing that this suggested a way to solve the problem, he immediately leapt from the tub in delight and went home naked, proclaiming aloud that he had found what he sought. As he ran he repeatedly cried in Greek, εὕρηκα, εὕρηκα, “I have found it, I have found it”.

Latín

Tum vero ex eo inventionis ingressu duas fecisse dicitur massas aequo pondere, quo etiam fuerat corona, unam ex auro et alteram ex argento. Cum ita fecisset, vas amplum ad summa labra implevit aquae, in quo demisit argenteam massam. Cuius quanta magnitudo in vasum depressa est, tantum aquae effluxit. Traducción

Following this discovery, he is said to have made two masses equal in weight to the crown, one of gold and the other of silver. He filled a large vessel to its brim with water and lowered the silver mass into it. The water that overflowed corresponded to the volume of the immersed mass. After removing it, he measured in sextarii the water needed to restore the former level at the brim. In this way he established how a given weight of silver corresponded to a particular volume of water.

Make the column capable of turning, so that, as the figure rises and points to the hours with its rod, rotation of the column adjusts their shortening and lengthening for the appropriate month.

Winter clocks are also made in another way, which are called anaphoric and are completed by these methods.
