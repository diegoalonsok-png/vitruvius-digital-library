Vitr., De arch. IV.praef.1

Latín

Cum animadvertissem, imperator, plures de architectura praecepta voluminaque commentariorum non ordinata sed incepta uti particulas errabundas reliquisse, dignam et utilissimam rem putavi tantae disciplinae corpus ad perfectam ordinationem perducere et praescriptas in singulis voluminibus singulorum generum qualitates explicare. itaque, Caesar, primo volumine tibi de officio eius et quibus eruditum esse rebus architectum oporteat exposui. secundo de copiis materiae, e quibus aedificia constituuntur, disputavi. tertio autem de aedium sacrarum dispositionibus et de earum generum varietate quasque et quot habeant species earumque quae sunt in singulis generibus distributiones,

Traducción

Having observed, Emperor, that many had left the precepts and volumes of commentaries on architecture not set in order but merely begun, like scattered fragments, I judged it a worthy and most useful thing to bring the body of so great a discipline to a perfect ordering, and to set forth, in each volume, the prescribed qualities of each of the kinds. And so, Caesar, in the first book I expounded to you the office of the architect and in what subjects he ought to be trained; in the second I treated of the materials from which buildings are constructed; and in the third, of the arrangement of sacred temples, of the variety of their kinds, how many and what species they have, and what in each kind are their distributions.

Vitr., De arch. IV.1.1

Latín

Columnae corinthiae praeter capitula omnes symmetrias habent uti ionicae, sed capitulorum altitudines efficiunt eas pro rata excelsiores et graciliores, quod ionici capituli altitudo tertia pars est crassitudinis columnae, corinthii tota crassitudo scapi. Igitur quod duae partes e crassitudine corinthiarum adiciuntur, efficiunt excelsitate speciem earum graciliorem.

Traducción

Corinthian columns have, except for their capitals, all the symmetries of the Ionic; but the height of their capitals makes them proportionally more slender and lofty, for the height of the Ionic capital is a third part of the column's thickness, whereas that of the Corinthian equals the whole thickness of the shaft. Thus, since two thirds of the thickness are added to the height of the Corinthian, their greater loftiness gives them a more graceful aspect.

Vitr., De arch. IV.1.2

Latín

cetera membra quae supra columnas inponuntur aut e doricis symmetriis aut ionicis moribus in corinthiis columnis conlocantur, quod ipsum corinthium genus propriam coronarum reliquorumque ornamentorum non habuerat institutionem, sed aut e triglyphorum rationibus mutuli in coronis et epistyliis guttae dorico more disponuntur, aut ex ionicis institutis zophoroe scalpturis ornati cum denticulis et coronis distribuuntur.

Traducción

The other members placed above the columns are arranged on Corinthian columns either by Doric symmetries or by Ionic usages, for the Corinthian order itself had no proper system of cornices and other ornaments: either, in the Doric manner, mutules are set in the cornices and guttae in the architraves, following the rules of the triglyphs; or, by Ionic usage, they are arranged with sculptured friezes (zophoroe), dentils, and cornices.

Vitr., De arch. IV.1.6

Latín

in ea aede cum voluissent columnas conlocare, non habentes symmetrias earum et quaerentes quibus rationibus efficere possent uti et ad onus ferendum essent idoneae et in aspectu probatam haberent venustatem, dimensi sunt virilis pedis vestigium et id retulerunt in altitudinem. cum invenissent pedem sextam partem esse altitudinis in homine, item in columnam transtulerunt et qua crassitudine fecerunt basim scapi, tantas sex cum capitulo in altitudinem extulerunt. ita dorica columna virilis corporis proportionem et firmitatem et venustatem in aedificiis praestare coepit.

Traducción

When they wished to set columns in that temple, not knowing their symmetries and seeking by what rules they might make them at once fit to bear the load and of approved beauty to the sight, they measured the print of a man's foot and referred it to the height. Finding that the foot is a sixth part of a man's height, they carried the same over to the column, and, with the thickness they gave to the base of the shaft, raised it six times that measure in height, including the capital. Thus the Doric column began to furnish in buildings the proportion, firmness, and beauty of the male body.

Vitr., De arch. IV.1.7

Latín

Item postea Dianae ⟨cum cogitarent⟩ constituere aedem quaerentes novi generis speciem, isdem vestigiis ad muliebrem transtulerunt gracilitatem, et fecerunt primum columnae crassitudinem altitudinis octava parte, ut haberet speciem excelsiorem. basi spiram supposuerunt pro calceo, capitulo volutas uti capillamento concrispatos cincinnos praependentes dextra ac sinistra conlocaverunt et cymatiis et encarpis pro crinibus dispositis frontes ornaverunt truncoque toto strias uti stolarum rugas matronali more demiserunt. ita duobus discriminibus columnarum inventionem, unam virili sine ornatu nuda specie, alteram muliebri subtilitate et ornatu symmetriaque sunt mutuati.

Traducción

Afterwards, wishing to raise a temple to Diana in the aspect of a new order, they turned by the same footprints to feminine slenderness, and first made the thickness of the column an eighth part of its height, that it might have a loftier look. Beneath the base they set a base-molding (spira) in place of a shoe; on the capital they placed volutes hanging to right and left like curled ringlets of hair, and adorned the fronts with cymatia and festoons (encarpi) arranged like locks; and down the whole shaft they let flutes fall like the folds of matrons' robes. Thus by two distinct criteria they took the invention of columns: one of male aspect, bare and without ornament; the other of feminine delicacy, ornament, and symmetry.

posteri vero elegantia subtilitateque iudiciorum progressi et gracilioribus modulis delectati septem crassitudinis diametros in altitudinem columnae doricae, ionicae novem constituerunt. id autem <genus>, quod Iones fecerunt primo, Ionicum est nominatum. Vitr., De arch. IV.1.9

Latín

eius autem capituli prima inventio sic memoratur esse facta. virgo civis Corinthia iam matura nuptiis inplicata morbo decessit. post sepulturam eius, quibus ea virgo viva pupulis delectabatur, nutrix collecta et composita in calatho pertulit ad monumentum et in summo conlocavit et, uti ea permanerent diutius subdiu, tegula texit. is calathus fortuito supra acanthi radicem fuerat conlocatus. interim pondere pressa radix acanthi media folia et cauliculos circum vernum tempus profudit, cuius cauliculi secundum calathi latera crescentes et ab angulis tegulae ponderis necessitate expressi flexuras in extremas partes volutarum facere sunt coacti.

Traducción

The first invention of this capital is recorded to have come about thus. A maiden, a citizen of Corinth, already of marriageable age, was carried off by illness. After her burial, her nurse gathered the little cups in which the girl had delighted while alive, arranged them in a basket (calathus), carried it to the tomb, and set it on top; and, that they might last longer in the open air, she covered it with a tile. The basket had by chance been placed over the root of an acanthus.

Vitr., De arch. IV.1.10

Latín

tunc Callimachus, qui propter elegantiam et subtilitatem artis marmoreae ab Atheniensibus κατατηξίτεχνος () fuerat nominatus, praeteriens hoc monumentum animadvertit eum calathum et circa foliorum nascentem teneritatem, delectatusque genere et formae novitate ad id exemplar columnas apud Corinthios fecit symmetriasque constituit, et ex eo in operis perfectionibus Corinthii generis distribuit rationes.

Traducción

Meanwhile the acanthus root, pressed down by the weight, put forth about springtime leaves and little stalks through the middle; and its stalks, growing up along the sides of the basket and forced by the weight of the tile's corners, were constrained to form volutes at their ends. Then Callimachus, whom the Athenians had named katatexitechnos (κατατηξίτεχνος, 'one who refines art to the point of consuming it') for the elegance and subtlety of his work in marble, passing by this monument, noticed the basket and the tender foliage growing about it; and, delighted by the novelty of the kind and form, he made columns after that model among the Corinthians and established their symmetries, and thenceforth distributed the rules of the Corinthian order in the execution of works.

Vitr., De arch. IV.1.11

Latín

eius autem capituli symmetria sic est facienda uti quanta fuerit crassitudo imae columnae, tanta sit altitudo capituli cum abaco. abaci latitudo ita habeat rationem ut quanta fuerit altitudo, tanta duo sint diagonia ab angulo ad angulum spatia. ita enim iustas habebunt frontes quoquoversus latitudines. frontes sinuentur introrsus ab extremis angulis abaci suae frontis latitudinis nona. imum capituli tantam habeat crassitudinem, quantam habet summa columna praeter apothesim et astragalum. abaci crassitudo septima capituli altitudinis.

Traducción

The symmetry of this capital is to be made so that its height, including the abacus, equals the thickness of the lower part of the column. Let the width of the abacus be in such proportion that its two diagonals, from angle to angle, are twice the height; for thus the fronts will have, on every side, the just width. Let the fronts be curved inward, from the outer angles of the abacus, by a ninth part of the width of their front. At its lower part let the capital have as much thickness as the upper part of the column, apart from the apothesis and the astragal. The thickness of the abacus is a seventh part of the capital's height.

Vitr., De arch. IV.1.12

Latín

dempta abaci crassitudine, dividatur reliqua pars in partes tres, e quibus una imo folio detur. secundum folium mediam altitudinem teneat. coliculi eandem habeant altitudinem, e quibus folia nascuntur proiecta uti excipiant quae ex coliculis natae procurrunt ad extremos angulos volutae, minoresque helices infra sinum medium qui est in abaco ⟨sub⟩ flores subiecti scalpantur. flores in quattuor partibus quanta erit abaci crassitudo tam magni formentur. ita his symmetriis corinthia capitula suas habebunt exactiones. Sunt autem quae isdem columnis inponuntur capitulorum genera variis vocabulis nominata, quorum nec proprietates symmetriarum nec columnarum genus aliud nominare possumus, sed ipsorum vocabula traducta et commutata ex corinthiis et pulvinatis et doricis videmus, quorum symmetriae sunt in novarum scalpturarum translatae subtilitatem.

Traducción

The thickness of the abacus being subtracted, let the remaining part be divided into three parts, of which one is given to the lower leaf; let the second leaf occupy the middle height; let the stalks (cauliculi) have the same height, from which the projecting leaves spring that receive the volutes which, born of the stalks, run out to the extreme angles, while the smaller helices are carved within their middle—that which is on the abacus—beneath the flower. Let the flowers, on the four sides, be made as large as the thickness of the abacus. Thus, by these symmetries, the Corinthian capitals will have their due proportions.

Vitr., De arch. IV.2.1

Latín

Quoniam autem de generibus columnarum origines et inventiones supra sunt scriptae, non alienum mihi videtur isdem rationibus de ornamentis eorum quemadmodum sunt prognata et quibus principiis et originibus inventa dicere. in aedificiis omnibus insuper conlocatur materiatio variis vocabulis nominata. ea autem uti in nominationibus, ita in re varias habet utilitates. trabes enim supra columnas et parastaticas et antas ponuntur, in contignationibus tigna et axes. sub tectis, si maiora spatia sunt, et transtra et capreoli, si commoda, columen et cantherii prominentes ad extremam suggrundationem. supra cantherios templa, deinde insuper sub tegulas asseres ita prominentes uti parietes proiecturis eorum tegantur.

Traducción

But since the origins and inventions of the kinds of columns have been set out above, it does not seem out of place to treat in the same way of their ornaments: how they arose and from what principles and origins they derive. In every building there is set, above, a framework of timber called by various names; and this framework, just as it varies in its names, so too it varies in its uses. For the beams (trabes) rest upon the columns, the pilasters (parastaticae) and the antae; in the floor-framing are placed the joists (tigna) and boards; under the roofs, where the spans are larger, the cross-beams (transtra) and the braces (capreoli); where they are moderate, the ridge-beam (columen) and the rafters (cantherii) that project to the edge of the eaves (suggrundatio); over the rafters, the purlins (templa); and then, above, beneath the tiles, the laths (asseres) projecting so that the walls are covered by their overhang.

Vitr., De arch. IV.2.2

Latín

ita unaquaeque res et locum et genus et ordinem proprium tuetur. e quibus rebus et a materiatura fabrili in lapideis et marmoreis aedium sacrarum aedificationibus artifices dispositiones eorum scalpturis sunt imitati et eas inventiones persequendas putaverunt. ideo quod antiqui fabri quodam in loco aedificantes, cum ita ab interioribus parietibus ad extremas partes tigna prominentia habuissent conlocata, inter tigna struxerunt supraque coronas et fastigia venustiore specie fabrilibus operibus ornaverunt, tum proiecturas tignorum quantum eminebant ad lineam et perpendiculum parietum praesecuerunt, quae species cum invenusta îs visa esset tabellas ita formatas uti nunc fiunt triglyphi contra tignorum praecisiones in fronte fixerunt et eas cera caerulea depinxerunt ut praecisiones tignorum tectae non offenderent visum. ita divisiones triglyphorum et ⟨met⟩oparum tignorum dispositione et intertigniûm habere in doricis operibus coeperunt.

Traducción

Thus each thing keeps its own place, kind, and order. Hence, taking their model from timber carpentry, the craftsmen imitated in their carvings—in the stone and marble construction of temples—the arrangement of those members, and judged that such discoveries should be followed. For the ancient carpenters, building in a certain place, once they had set the beams (tigna) projecting from the inner walls out to the outermost parts, raised their structure between the beams and, above, adorned the cornices and pediments with carpentry of more graceful aspect; then they cut back the projections of the beams, as far as they jutted out, to the line and the vertical of the walls. And since that aspect seemed to them ungraceful, they fixed on the front, against the cut ends of the beams, small tablets shaped as triglyphs are now made, and painted them with blue wax, so that the cut ends of the beams, thus covered, should not offend the eye. So, imitating the divisions of the beams, they began to have in Doric works the arrangement of triglyphs and, between beam and beam, the metopes.

Vitr., De arch. IV.2.3

Latín

postea alii in aliis operibus ad perpendiculum triglyphorum cantherios prominentes proiecerunt eorumque proiecturas simaverunt. ex eo uti tignorum dispositionibus triglyphi, ita e cantheriorum proiecturis mutulorum sub coronis ratio est inventa. ita fere in operibus lapideis et marmoreis mutuli inclinatis scalpturis deformantur, quod imitatio est cantheriorum. etenim necessario propter stillicidia proclinati conlocantur. ergo et triglyphorum et mutulorum in doricis operibus ratio ex ea imitatione inventa est.

Traducción

Afterwards others, in other works, carried the projecting rafters out to the vertical of the triglyphs and gave their projections a cyma (sima) profile. Hence, just as the triglyphs arose from the arrangement of the joists, so from the projections of the rafters the principle of the mutules (mutuli) beneath the cornices was found. Thus, generally, in works of stone and marble the mutules are carved with an inward slope, since they are an imitation of the rafters; for these are necessarily set sloping on account of the rain-drip (stillicidia). Therefore the principle of both the triglyphs and the mutules in Doric works was discovered from that imitation.

Vitr., De arch. IV.2.4

Latín

Non enim quemadmodum nonnulli errantes dixerunt fenestrarum imagines esse triglyphos ita potest esse, quod in angulis contraque tetrantes columnarum triglyphi constituuntur, quibus in locis omnino non patitur res fenestras fieri. dissolvuntur enim angulorum in aedificiis iuncturae, si in îs fenestrarum fuerint lumina relicta. etiamque ubi nunc triglyphi constituuntur si ibi luminum spatia fuisse iudicabuntur, isdem rationibus denticuli in ionicis fenestrarum occupavisse loca videbuntur. utraque enim et inter denticulos et inter triglyphos quae sunt intervalla metopae nominantur. ὀπαι enim Graeci tignorum cubicula et asserum appellant, uti nostri ea cava columbaria. ita quod inter duas opas est intertignium, id μετόπη est apud eos nominata Ita.

Traducción

For it cannot be, as some have said in error, that triglyphs are images of windows; because triglyphs are set at the corners and opposite the quarter-points (tetrantes) of the columns—places where it is by no means possible to make windows. For the joints at the corners of buildings would be broken apart if window-openings were left in them. And further, if it were judged that, where triglyphs now stand, there had once been openings for light, then by the same reasoning the dentils in the Ionic would be supposed to have occupied the place of windows. For in both cases the intervals—both those between the dentils and those between the triglyphs—are called metopes. For the Greeks call the sockets (cubilia) of the beams and of the laths ὀπαί, just as ours call those hollows columbaria. Thus what lies between two ὀπαί, the inter-beam space, they name μετόπη.

Vitr., De arch. IV.2.5

Latín

Uti autem in doricis triglyphorum et mutulorum est inventa ratio, item in ionicis denticulorum constitutio propriam in operibus habet rationem, et quemadmodum mutuli cantheriorum proiecturae ferunt imaginem, sic in ionicis denticuli ex proiecturis asserum habent imitationem. itaque in graecis operibus nemo sub mutulo denticulos constituit. non enim possunt subtus cantherios asseres esse. quod ergo supra cantherios et templa in veritate debet esse conlocatum, id in imaginibus si infra constitutum fuerit mendosam habebit operis rationem. etiam quod antiqui non probaverunt neque instituerunt in fastigiis <mutulos aut> denticulos fieri sed puras coronas, ideo quod nec cantherii nec asseres contra fastigiorum frontes distribuuntur nec possunt prominere, sed ad stillicidia proclinati conlocantur. ita quod non potest in veritate fieri, id non putaverunt in imaginibus factum posse certam rationem habere.

Traducción

And just as in the Doric the principle of triglyphs and mutules was found, so in the Ionic the disposition of the dentils has its own basis in the works; and just as the mutules reproduce the image of the projections of the rafters, so in the Ionic the dentils imitate the projections of the laths (asseres). For this reason, in Greek works no one sets dentils beneath mutules, since laths cannot lie beneath rafters. So what in reality must be set above the rafters and the purlin, if in the images it is placed below, will give the work a faulty design. The ancients, moreover, did not approve or establish the making of ⟨mutules or⟩ dentils on the pediments, but only plain cornices, because neither the rafters nor the laths are distributed against the fronts of the pediments, nor can they project, but are set sloping towards the drip. Thus, what cannot be done in reality, they judged could have no sound design when done in the images.

Vitr., De arch. IV.2.6

Latín

omnia enim certa proprietate et a veris naturae deducta moribus transduxerunt in operum perfectiones, et ea probaverunt quorum explicationes in disputationibus rationem possunt habere veritatis. itaque ex eis originibus symmetrias et proportiones uniuscuiusque generis constitutas reliquerunt. quorum ingressus persecutus de ionicis et corinthiis institutionibus supra dixi, nunc vero doricam rationem summamque eius speciem breviter exponam.

Traducción

For they carried over all things into the perfection of their works with sure propriety and drawn from the true laws of nature, and they approved only those things whose explanations can uphold, in debate, the principle of truth. And so, from those origins, they left the symmetries and proportions of each kind established. Having traced their development, I have spoken above of the Ionic and Corinthian rules; now, however, I shall briefly set out the Doric system and the general character of its appearance.

Vitr., De arch. IV.3.1

Latín

Nonnulli antiqui architecti negaverunt dorico genere aedes sacras oportere fieri, quod mendosae et disconvenientes in his symmetriae conficiebantur. itaque negavit Arcesius, item Pytheos, non minus Hermogenes. nam is cum paratam habuisset marmoris copiam in doricae aedis perfectionem, <> commutavit ex eadem copia et eam ionicam Libero Patri fecit. sed tamen non quod invenusta est species aut genus aut formae dignitas, sed quod inpedita est distributio et incommoda in opere triglyphorum et lacunariorum [distributione].

Traducción

Some ancient architects denied that sacred buildings should be built in the Doric style, because they created false and inappropriate symmetries. And so Arcesius denied it, Pythius as well, and Hermogenes no less. For when he had prepared a supply of marble for the completion of the Doric building, he changed it from the same supply and made it Ionic for the Free Father. However, it is not that the species or genus or dignity of the form are unattractive, but rather that the distribution is complicated and inconvenient in the arrangement of the triglyphs and ceiling work.

Vitr., De arch. IV.3.2

Latín

namque necesse est triglyphos constitui contra medios tetrantes columnarum, metopasque quae inter triglyphos fient aeque longas esse quam altas. contraque in angulares columnas triglyphi in extremis partibus constituuntur et non contra medios tetrantes. ita metopae, quae proximae ad angulares triglyphos fiunt, non exeunt quadratae sed oblongiores triglyphi dimidia latitudine. at qui metopas aequales volunt facere, intercolumnia extrema contrahunt triglyphi dimidia latitudine. hoc autem sive metoparum longitudinibus sive intercolumniorum contractionibus efficietur, est mendosum. quapropter antiqui vitare visi sunt in aedibus sacris doricae symmetriae rationem.

Traducción

For the triglyphs must be set opposite the middle quarter-points (tetrantes) of the columns, and the metopes between the triglyphs must be as long as they are high. But at the corner columns the triglyphs are placed at the ends, not opposite the middle points; and so the metopes nearest the corner triglyphs come out not square but oblong, broader by half the width of a triglyph. Those who wish to make the metopes equal contract the outermost intercolumniations by half the width of a triglyph. item latitudo dividatur in partes X. ex his ternae partes dextra ac sinistra cellis minoribus sive ibi alae futurae sunt dentur, reliquae quattuor mediae aedi attribuantur. spatium quod erit ante cellas in pronao, ita columnis designetur ut angulares contra antas, parietum extremorum e regione, conlocentur, duae mediae e regione parietum qui inter antas et mediam aedem fuerint, ita distribuantur, et inter antas et columnas priores per medium isdem regionibus alterae disponantur. eaeque sint ima crassitudine altitudinis parte VII, altitudo tertia parte latitudinis templi, summaque columna quarta parte crassitudinis imae contrahatur.

Traducción

Likewise, let the breadth be divided into ten parts. Of these, three on the right and three on the left be given to the lesser cellae—or to the wings, if there be any; the remaining four, in the middle, be assigned to the temple. The space before the cellae, in the pronaos, be marked out with columns so that the corner ones stand opposite the antae, in the line of the outermost walls; the two central ones in the line of the walls that lie between the antae and the middle of the temple; and between the antae and the front columns, through the middle, let others be set in those same lines. Let these be, at the bottom, of a thickness equal to a seventh part of their height; the height a third part of the breadth of the temple; and let the top of the column be drawn in by a fourth part of the lower thickness.

Vitr., De arch. IV.7.3

Latín

Spirae earum altae dimidia parte crassitudinis fiant. habeant spirae earum plinthum ad circinum, altam suae crassitudinis dimidia parte, torum insuper cum apophysi crassum quantum plinthus. capituli altitudo dimidia crassitudinis. abaci latitudo quanta ima crassitudo columnae. capitulique crassitudo dividatur in partes tres, e quibus una plintho, quae est abacus, detur, altera echino, tertia hypotrachelio cum apophysi.

Traducción

Let their bases (spirae) be half as high as their thickness. Let the bases have a circular plinth, half as high as their thickness, and above it a torus with its apophyge, as thick as the plinth. The height of the capital, half the thickness. The width of the abacus, equal to the lower thickness of the column. Let the thickness of the capital be divided into three parts: one given to the plinth, which is on the abacus; another to the echinus; and the third to the hypotrachelion with its apophyge.

Vitr., De arch. IV.7.4

Latín

Supra columnas trabes compactiles inponantur ut altitudinis modulis îs, qui a magnitudine operis postulabuntur. eaeque trabes compactiles eam habeant crassitudinem, quanta summae columnae erit hypotrachelium, et ita sint compactae subscudibus et securiclis, ut compactura duorum digitorum habeant laxationem. cum enim inter se tangunt et non spiramentum et perflatum venti recipiunt, concalefaciuntur et celeriter putrescunt.

Traducción

Upon the columns let framed beams be placed, of a height in modules such as the magnitude of the work demands; and let these framed beams be so laid that they have a width equal to that of the tops of the columns, and be joined with tenons and dovetails, leaving in the joint a play of two fingers: for when they touch one another and admit no breath or draught of air, they grow hot and quickly rot.

Latín

supra trabes et supra parietes traiecturae mutulorum parte IIII altitudinis columnae proiciantur, item in eorum frontibus antepagmenta figantur, supraque îs tympanum fastigii structura seu de materia conlocetur. supraque eûm fastigium, columen, cantherii, templa ita sunt conlocanda ut stillicidium tecti absoluti tertiario respondeat.

Traducción

Above the beams and above the walls, let the projections of the mutules jut out a fourth part of the height of the column; and on their fronts let the door-casings (antepagmenta) be fixed. Above this let the tympanum of the pediment be set, of masonry or of timber; and above it the ridge-beam (columen), the rafters (cantherii), and the purlins (templa), so arranged that the drip of the finished roof answers to a third.

Vitr., De arch. IV.8.1

Latín

Fiunt autem aedes rotundae, e quibus aliae monopteroe sine cella columnatae constituuntur, aliae peripteroe dicuntur. quae sine cella fiunt, tribunal habent et ascensum ex sua diametro tertiae partis. insuper stylobatam columnae constituuntur tam altae quanta ab extremis parietibus est diametros stylobatarum, crassae altitudinis suae cum capitulis et spiris decumae partis. epistylium altum columnae crassitudinis dimidia parte. zophorum et reliqua quae insuper inponuntur, ita uti in III volumine de symmetriis scripsi.

Traducción

Round temples are also made, of which some, called monopteral, are built colonnaded without a cella, and others are called peripteral. Those made without a cella have a platform (tribunal) and an approach of stairs a third part of their diameter. Upon the stylobates columns are raised as tall as the interval between the outermost walls, and of a thickness equal to a tenth part of their height, including base and capital. The architrave (epistylium) is half the thickness of the column. The frieze (zophorus) and the rest that is set above, as I wrote in the third book on the symmetries.

Latín

Sin autem peripteros ea aedes constituetur, duo gradus et stylobata ab imo constituantur. Deinde cellae paries conlocetur cum recessu eius a stylobata circa partem latitudinis quintam, medioque valvarum locus ad aditus relinquatur; eaque cella tantam habeat diametrum praeter parietes et circumitionem, quantam altitudinem columna. Supra stylobatam columnae circum cellam isdem symmetriis, quae ⟨supra scriptae sunt,⟩ disponantur.

Traducción

If the temple is to be peripteral, provide two steps and the stylobate from the ground up. Set the cella wall approximately one-fifth of the width back from the stylobate, leaving an opening in the middle for the entrance. Excluding the walls and the surrounding passage, the cella’s diameter should equal the height of the column. Arrange the columns around the cella on the stylobate according to the proportions already given.

Latín

in medio tecti ratio ita habeatur uti quanta diametros totius operis erit futura dimidia altitudo fiat tholi praeter florem, flos autem tantam habeat magnitudinem quantam habuerit columnae capitulum, praeter pyramidem. reliqua uti supra scripta sunt, isdem proportionibus atque symmetriis facienda videntur.

Traducción

In the middle, let the plan of the roof be such that, whatever the diameter of the whole work is to be, half of it be the height of the tholos, apart from the finial (flos). Let the finial have as much size as the capital of the column, apart from the pyramid. The rest is to be made by the same symmetries and proportions as written above.

Vitr., De arch. IV.8.4

Latín

Item generibus aliis constituuntur aedes ex isdem symmetriis ordinatae et alio genere dispositiones habentes, uti est Castoris in circo Flaminio, et inter duos lucos Veiovis, item augustius Nemori Dianae columnis adiectis dextra ac sinistra ad umeros pronai. hoc autem genere primo facta est, uti est Castoris in circo Athenis in arce et in Attica Sunio Palladis Minervae. earum non aliae sed eaedem sunt proportiones. cellae enim longitudinibus duplices sunt ad latitudines uti reliquae, ex îs omnia quae solent esse in frontibus, ad latera sunt translata.

Traducción

Other kinds of temple follow the same proportions but employ a different arrangement: for example, the temple of Castor in the Circus Flaminius, that of Vediovis between the two sacred groves, and, on a grander scale, Diana’s temple in the Grove, with columns added on either side of the pronaos. The temples of Pallas Minerva on the Athenian Acropolis and at Sunium in Attica belong to the type exemplified by the temple of Castor in the Circus. Their proportions remain the same. As in the other temples, the cellae are twice as long as they are wide, but the elements normally placed at the front have been transferred to the sides.

Latín

nonnulli etiam de tuscanicis generibus sumentes columnarum dispositiones transferunt in corinthiorum et ionicorum operum ordinationes, et quibus in locis in pronao procurrunt antae, in isdem e regione cellae parietum columnas binas conlocantes efficiunt tuscanicorum et graecorum operum communem ratiocinationem.

Traducción

Some builders also take the column arrangements of Tuscan temples and apply them to Corinthian and Ionic compositions. Where the antae project into the pronaos, they place paired columns in line with the cella walls, producing a design that combines Tuscan and Greek principles.

Vitr., De arch. IV.8.6

Latín

alii vero removentes parietes aedis et adplicantes ad intercolumnia, pteromatos spatiis sublati efficiunt amplum laxamentum cellae, reliqua autem proportionibus et symmetriis isdem conservantes aliud genus figurae nominisque videntur pseudoperipterum procreavisse. haec autem genera propter usum sacrificiorum convertuntur. non enim omnibus diis isdem rationibus aedes sunt faciundae, quod alius alia varietate sacrorum religionum habet effectus.

Traducción

Others move the temple walls outward to meet the intercolumniations. By eliminating the surrounding pteroma they obtain a spacious cella, while retaining the other proportions and symmetries. In doing so they seem to have created a type with a different form and name, the pseudoperipteral temple. These types vary according to sacrificial practice: temples should not be designed on the same principles for every deity, since the effect of each cult depends on its particular rites.

Latín

Omnes aedium sacrarum ratiocinationes uti mihi traditae sunt exposui ordinesque et symmetrias earum partitionibus distinxi, et quarum dispares sunt figurae et quibus discriminibus inter se sunt disparatae quoad potui significare scriptis exposui. nunc de aris deorum inmortalium uti aptam constitutionem habeant ad sacrificiorum rationem dicam.

Traducción

I have explained all the reasoning of the sacred buildings, as they have been handed down to me, and I have distinguished their orders and symmetries by their divisions, and whose disparate figures they are, and by what distinctions they arise from one another. I explained to another, as far as I could express in writing. Now I will speak of the areas of the immortal gods, so that they may have a suitable constitution as the object of sacrifices.

Vitr., De arch. IV.9.1

Latín

Arae spectent ad orientem et semper inferiores sint conlocatae quam simulacra, quae fuerint in aede, uti suspicientes divinitatem, qui supplicant, et sacrificent. Disparibus altitudinibus ad sui cuiusque dei decorem componantur. Altitudines autem earum sic sunt explicandae, uti Iovi omnibusque caelestibus quam excelsissimae constituantur, Vestae Terrae Matrique humiles conlocentur. Ita idoneae his institutionibus explicabuntur modulationibus ararum deformationes. Explicatis aedium sacrarum compositionibus in hoc libro, insequenti de communium operum reddemus distributionibus explicationes.

Traducción

Altars should face east and always stand below the cult images within the temple, so that those who pray and sacrifice look upward towards the deity. Their heights should vary according to the decor appropriate to each god. For Jupiter and all the celestial gods, make the altars as high as possible; for Vesta, Earth and the Mother, set them low. These principles yield altar forms appropriate in their proportions.
