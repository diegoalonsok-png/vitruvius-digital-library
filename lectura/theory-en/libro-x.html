Latín

Nobili Graecorum et ampla civitate Ephesi lex vetusta dicitur a maioribus dura condicione sed iure esse non iniquo constituta. Nam architectus, cum publicum opus curandum recipit, pollicetur, quanto sumptu id sit futurum. Tradita aestimatione magistratui bona eius obligantur, donec opus sit perfectum. Absoluto autem, cum ad dictum inpensa respondit, decretis et honoribus ornatur. Item si non amplius quam quarta, ad aestimationem est adicienda, de publico praestatur, neque ulla poena tenetur. Cum vero amplius quam quarta in opere consumitur, ex eius bonis ad perficiendum pecunia exigitur.

Traducción

In the great and distinguished Greek city of Ephesus, the ancestors are said to have established an ancient law, severe in its terms yet not unjust. When an architect undertakes a public building, he declares its estimated cost. Once that estimate has been submitted to the magistrate, his property stands as security until the work is complete. If the final expenditure agrees with the estimate, he receives public honors and decrees of recognition. An overrun of no more than one quarter is covered by the public treasury without penalty. If the excess exceeds one quarter, however, the additional money required to complete the work is recovered from the architect’s own property.
