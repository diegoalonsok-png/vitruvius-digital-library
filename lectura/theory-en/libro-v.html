Latín

Qui amplioribus voluminibus, imperator, ingenii cogitationes praeceptaque explicaverunt, maximas et egregias adiecerunt suis scriptis auctoritates. quod etiam velim nostris quoque studiis res pateretur, ut amplificationibus auctoritas et in his praeceptis augeretur, sed id non est quemadmodum putatur expeditum. non enim de architectura sic scribitur uti historia aut poemata. historiae per se tenent lectores. habent enim novarum rerum varias expectationes. poematorum vero [carminum] metra et pedes ac verborum elegans dispositio et sententiarum inter personas distinctas [versuum] pronuntiatio prolectando sensus legentium perducit sine offensa ad summam scriptorum terminationem.

Traducción

Those who have expounded their ideas and precepts in more extensive volumes, Emperor, have lent their writings considerable distinction and authority. I too should wish the subject of our studies to permit such expansiveness, so that greater length might enhance the authority of these teachings; but this is less straightforward than one might suppose. Architecture cannot be written about in the same way as history or poetry. History holds the reader’s attention through the prospect of varied new events; poetry, through its meters, rhythms, elegant arrangement of words and speeches assigned to different characters, carries the reader’s attention smoothly to the conclusion of the work.

extra autem disponantur porticus tres, una ex peristylo exeuntibus, duae dextra atque sinistra stadiatae, ex quibus una quae spectaverit ad septentrionem, perficiatur duplex amplissima latitudine, altera simplex ita factae uti in partibus quae fuerint circa parietes et quae erit ad columnas, margines habeant uti semitas non minus pedum denûm mediumque excavatum uti gradus sint in descensu marginibus sesquipedem ad planitiem, quae planities sit non minus pedes XII. ita qui vestiti ambulaverint circum in marginibus, non inpedientur ab unctis se exercentibus.

Traducción

Outside, three porticos are to be arranged, one leading from the peristyle, and two on the right and left, of which one, which faces north, is to be completed as a double, with a very ample width, the other single, so made that in the parts which will be around the walls and which will be at the columns, the edges should be like paths of not less than ten feet and the middle should be excavated so that there are steps in the descent from the edges, one half-foot to the plain, which plain should be no less than twelve feet. Thus, those who walk around the edges dressed will not be hindered by the anointed exercising themselves.

haec autem porticus ξυστός apud Graecos vocitatur, quod athletae per hiberna tempora in tectis stadiis exercentur. proxime autem xystum et duplicem porticum designentur hypaethroe ambulationes, quas Graeci παραδρομίδες, nostri xysta appellant, in quas per hiemem ex xysto sereno caelo athletae prodeuntes exercentur. faciunda autem xysta sic videntur ut sint inter duas porticus silvae aut platanones et in his perficiantur inter arbores ambulationes ibique ex opere signino stationes. post xystum autem stadium ita figuratum ut possint hominum copiae cum laxamento athletas certantes spectare. Quae in moenibus necessaria videbantur esse ut apte disponantur, perscripsi.

Traducción

This portico is called the ξυστός among the Greeks, because athletes exercise in covered stadia during the winter months. Next to the xystum and the double portico are designated the hypaethroe walks, which the Greeks call παραδρομίδας, ours xysta, in which athletes, coming out of the xystum in clear weather, exercise during the winter. The xystum is made in such a way that there are forests or plane trees between the two porticos and in these walks are made between the trees and there are stations according to the work I design. After the xystum is the stadium, so shaped that crowds of people can watch the athletes competing with ease. I have written down what seemed to be necessary in the walls to be arranged appropriately.

Vitr., De arch. V.12.1

Latín

de opportunitate autem portuum non est praetermittendum, sed quibus rationibus tueantur naves in his ab tempestatibus explicandum. hi autem naturaliter si sint bene positi habeantque acroteria sive promunturia procurrentia, ex quibus introrsus curvaturae sive versurae ex loci natura fuerint conformatae, maximas utilitates videntur habere. circum enim porticus sive navalia sunt facienda sive ex porticibus aditus ad emporia, turresque ex utraque parte conlocandae, ex quibus catenae traduci per machinas possint.

Traducción

The suitability of harbors must also be considered, together with the means of protecting ships from storms. Natural sites offer great advantages when projecting headlands form inward-curving bays or sheltered angles. Build porticoes or shipyards around them, with access from the porticoes to the trading facilities. Place towers on both sides of the entrance, from which chains can be drawn across by machinery.

Latín

Sin autem non naturalem locum neque idoneum ad tuendas ab tempestatibus naves habuerimus, ita videtur esse faciendum uti si nullum flumen in his locis inpedierit sed erit ex una parte statio, tunc ex altera parte structuris sive aggeribus expediantur progressus. et ita conformandae portuum conclusiones. eae autem structurae quae in aqua sunt futurae, videntur sic esse faciendae uti portetur pulvis a regionibus quae sunt a Cumis continuatae ad promunturium Minervae, isque misceatur uti in mortario duo ad unum respondeant.

Traducción

If there is no natural site suitable for sheltering ships from storms, proceed as follows. Where no river obstructs the site and there is an anchorage on one side, extend masonry works or embankments from the other to form the harbor enclosure. For structures to be built in water, bring volcanic powder from the region extending from Cumae to the promontory of Minerva and mix it into mortar in a ratio of two parts powder to one part lime.

deinde tunc in eo loco qui definitus erit, arcae stipitibus robusteis et catenis inclusae in aquam demittendae destinandaeque firmiter, deinde inter ea extrastilis inferior pars sub aqua exaequanda et purganda, et caementis ex mortario, materia mixta quemadmodum supra scriptum est, ibi congerendum, doneque compleatur structurae spatium quod fuerit inter arcas. hoc autem munus naturale habent ea loca quae supra scripta sunt. Traducción

Then, in the place that will be determined, the caissons (arcae), framed with stout posts and chains, are to be lowered into the water and firmly placed, then the lower part inside the water is to be leveled and cleaned with the help of the ladles, and rubble and mortar, the material mixed as described above, are to be piled there, until the structure is completed, filling the space between the arks. Now, the places described above have this natural function. But if, due to waves or the force of the open sea, the arks cannot be contained, then a cushion is to be built as firmly as possible from the land itself or from the shore, and this cushion, when leveled, is to be built so that less than half of the surface is level, the remainder, which is closest to the shore, has a sloping side.

deinde ad ipsam aquam et latera pulvino circiter sesquipedales margines struantur aequilibres ex planitia quae est supra scripta, tunc proclinatio ea impleatur harena et exaequetur cum margine et planitia pulvini. deinde insuper eam exaequationem pila quam magna constituta fuerit ibi struatur, eaque cum erit extructa, relinquatur ne minus duos menses ut siccescat. tunc autem succidatur margo quae sustinet harenam. ita harena fluctibus subruta efficiet in mare pilae praecipitationem. hac ratione quotienscumque opus fuerit, in aquam poterit esse progressus.

Traducción

Then, to the water itself and the sides of the cushion, margins of about one and a half feet are built, equal to the level described above. Then, that slope is filled with sand and made equal to the edge and level of the cushion. Then, on top of that leveling, a large pile is built there, and when it is built, it is left for at least two months to dry. Then, however, the edge that supports the sand is cut away. Thus, the sand, crushed by the waves, will cause the pile to precipitate into the sea. In this way, whenever necessary, it will be possible to advance into the water.

Latín

In quibus autem locis pulvis non nascitur, his rationibus erit faciendum uti arcae duplices relatis tabulis et catenis conligatae in eo loco qui finitus erit constituantur, et inter destinas creta in aeronibus ex ulva palustri factis calcetur. cum ita bene calcatum et quam densissime fuerit, tunc cocleis rotis tympanis conlocatis locus qui ea saeptione finitus fuerit exinaniatur sicceturque, et ibi inter saeptiones fundamenta fodiantur. si terrena erunt, usque ad solidum crassiora quam qui murus supra futurus erit, et tunc structura ex caementis calce et harena compleantur.

Traducción

In places where pozzolana (pulvis) is not found, it will be necessary to do this by placing double caissons, tied together with burnt sticks and chains, in the place that will be finished, and between the designated places, chalk will be trampled in aerofoils made of marsh bulrush. When it has been trampled well and as densely as possible, then with augers and drum wheels placed, the place that will be finished with that partition will be emptied and dried, and there between the partitions the foundations will be dug. If they will be earthen, they will be emptied and dried until solid, thicker than the wall that will be above, and then the structure will be completed with rubble, lime, and sand.

Quae necessaria ad utilitatem in civitatibus publicorum locorum succurrere mihi potuerunt, quemadmodum constituantur et perficiantur in hoc volumine scripsi, privatorum autem aedificiorum utilitates et eorum symmetrias insequenti volumine ratiocinabor

Traducción

When these are completed, the plan for the shipyards will be to build those facing mainly to the north. For the southern regions, because of the heat, breed and nourish moths, beetles, and other kinds of harmful animals, and those buildings should be least materialized because of fires. There should be no limitation on the sizes, but the maximum size of the ships should be made, so that even if larger ships are removed, they can still be accommodated there with ease. I have written in this volume how these necessary utilities in the cities of public places can be of assistance to me, but I will discuss the utilities and their symmetries of private buildings in the following volume.
