Suggested directory description

Vitruvius Digital Library is an independent, freely accessible digital research collection devoted to Vitruvius' De architectura, Latin textual tradition, historical editions, manuscript provenance, architectural vocabulary and comparative reading. It provides locus-oriented reading and search tools, references to institutional facsimiles and historically situated editorial materials, and access to related Greek and Latin resources. Its author's V514, V554 and V297 study publications have separate Zenodo DOIs. The project differentiates editorial transcriptions, modern translations, external digitised witnesses and machine-derived OCR. It is a research aid, not a completed comprehensive critical collation or a formally peer-reviewed edition.

Suggested directory description

Vitruvia is the developing humanities-oriented search interface associated with Vitruvius Digital Library. It supports discovery of indexed passages, bibliographic materials, classical terminology and related resources, with links back to documented sources wherever available. The interface is in beta: indexing, ranking and experimental semantic or external retrieval functions require independent validation. Literal results, editorial interpretations and external references should not be treated as equivalent evidentiary categories.

Rights and methodological notice

Free access is not the same as permission to redistribute. Diego A. Kurilo retains applicable rights to his original books, translations, commentary and editorial material; their free online reading availability does not itself grant unrestricted reuse. Third-party texts, images, manuscript facsimiles and institutional digitizations retain their own applicable rights and source attributions. The repository is publicly inspectable; the applicable code licence must be checked separately, and no blanket open-source licence is represented here.

Stable DOIs identify publication records; they do not by themselves certify peer review. Textual concordances, manuscript-to-locus associations and OCR readings must be checked against their sources.
